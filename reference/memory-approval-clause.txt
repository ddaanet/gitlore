Before drafting the summary, invoke the `gitlore:memory-writing` skill and hold
every pending memory change against it: whether the fact belongs in the store at
all, what its body claims, which tier it sits in, and whether its index line
carries the literal a future session arrives holding. Land those fixes first —
the summary describes the facts as they will be committed.

The commit message is a subject line, a blank line, then one paragraph per
changed memory file. Each paragraph opens with a bold prefix naming the file's
kind — New, Update, Augment, Reduce, or Remove — and its tier/slug, then says
in prose what the fact now claims and what prompted the change. A MEMORY.md —
root or tier — gets no paragraph: an index line moves with the fact it points
at, so its changes are already implied. Template:

    <subject line, no conventional-commit prefix>

    **New <tier>/<slug>:** what the fact records, and what prompted writing it
    now rather than earlier.

    **Update <tier>/<slug>:** what the wording claimed before, what it claims
    now, and the evidence that moved it.

    **Remove <tier>/<slug>:** what it claimed, and what showed it was wrong or
    redundant.
