Before drafting the summary, invoke the `gitlore:memory-writing` skill and hold
every pending memory change against it: whether the fact belongs in the store at
all, what its body claims, which tier it sits in, and whether its index line
carries the literal a future session arrives holding. Land those fixes first —
the summary describes the facts as they will be committed.

The commit message is a subject line, a blank line, then one paragraph per
changed memory file, one to three sentences each. A paragraph opens with a bold
prefix naming the file's kind — New, Update, Augment, Reduce, or Remove — and
its tier/slug, names the change in one clause, and spends the rest on what the
diff cannot show: what prompted it (the incident, correction or measurement,
with its date or version), what the old wording got wrong, and for a Reduce or
Remove where the content went. Do not retell the new body or the index line:
the edits are already in the conversation and in `git show`, and a retelling
buries the cause the approver is weighing. Attribute a decision or correction
to whoever made it. A MEMORY.md — root or tier — gets no paragraph. Template:

    <subject line, no conventional-commit prefix>

    **New <tier>/<slug>:** what it records, in one clause. What prompted
    writing it now.

    **Update <tier>/<slug>:** what changed, in one clause. What the old wording
    got wrong, and the evidence that showed it.

    **Remove <tier>/<slug>:** what showed it wrong or redundant, and where its
    content now lives.
